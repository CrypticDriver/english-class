🧠 答题心法（每题必读）

口语高分的根本顺序

1️⃣ 先有观点（Point first）
听到题先想清楚 “我的立场 / 态度是什么”——先亮出一句明确的观点，不要上来就堆句子。

2️⃣ 再造句子（Sentences follow）
观点定了，再用句式 + 词组去 支撑它（举例 / 解释 / 对比）。句子是为观点服务的，不是为了用而用。

一句话：先回答 “What do I think?”，再回答 “How do I say it?”——顺序反了就变成背句子，考官一听就知道。

🤔 为什么选这两题？

老师很可能会追问“你为什么选这两个话题”——提前想好答法，自然不卡壳

I picked these two because they both look at attention and connection, just from opposite directions. The focus question is about my relationship with myself — whether I can control my own mind when nothing else is forcing me to.

The client question is about my relationship with someone else — it's not about willpower, it's about whether two people's communication styles naturally line up. One is internal, one is interpersonal, but they're both really about "what makes something feel effortless versus something that takes constant effort."

中文备用（万一老师用中文问）：这两题都在探讨"专注/连接"，只是方向相反。专注那题关于我和自己的关系——没有外力逼着的时候能不能管住自己的思绪。客户那题关于我和别人的关系——不是靠意志力,是两个人的沟通方式是不是天生就合拍。一个是内在的,一个是人际的,但本质都是"什么东西让人觉得毫不费力,什么东西一直很费劲"。

📱 话题 ① 你容易分心还是可以很专注？

Do you think you're someone who gets distracted easily, or can you stay really focused? Do you constantly get pulled away by your phone, random thoughts, or noises — or can you shut everything out and focus on what's in front of you? — Everyday Life, 性格/习惯类话题

🎯 核心观点（选 2-3 个你最有共鸣的）

  • 要先分清"分心的类型"，而不是给自己贴一个标签 — 面对无聊/重复性的事很容易分心，但面对有挑战性、我在乎的事，反而可以进入很深的专注状态；所以不是"我是哪种人"，而是"这件事是哪种事"
  • 真正的问题不是"容易分心"，是"分心的诱因太容易触达" — 手机放在手边、消息不断弹出，不是我天生坐不住，是环境把打断这件事变得太方便了；把手机拿走，专注力立刻回来
  • 专注力是练出来的，不是天生的 — 刚入行时任何声音、任何消息都能把我拉走；现在处理客户问题时，能做到"进入状态"，屏蔽掉周围大部分噪音，这是这几年被工作逼出来的能力
  • 升华：越有"利害关系"的事，越容易专注 — 分心本质上是大脑在权衡"这件事重不重要"，重要到会有后果的事，大脑自然会分配全部注意力

📝 Part 2 完整范文

Cue card: Describe whether you find it easy to stay focused or you get distracted easily.

If I'm being honest, it really depends on what I'm doing. If it's something repetitive or a bit boring — like going through routine paperwork — I can get pulled away by literally anything: my phone buzzing, a random thought, even someone talking two desks over. But if it's something I actually care about, or something with a real deadline attached, I can lock in and completely lose track of time.

So I don't think the honest answer is "I'm a distracted person" or "I'm a focused person." I think the real issue is that distractions are just one buzz away — my phone sits right next to me all day, notifications keep popping up, and it's simply too easy to glance over. It's not that my mind can't hold still; it's that the environment makes interruption almost effortless.

What actually proves that to me is a small experiment I do sometimes: if I put my phone out of reach — like in another room — my focus comes back almost instantly. Nothing about my brain changed; I just removed the easiest exit route.

And when something has real stakes — a client issue that needs solving right now, a report due in an hour — I can shut everything else out without even trying. That tells me focus isn't really about willpower in the moment; it's about how much the task actually matters to me.

Looking back, this wasn't always true. Early in my career, almost anything could throw me off. So I'd say the ability to focus on demand is a skill I've built up over time, not something I was born with — and it's still very situational, not automatic.

🤝 话题 ② 有没有跟哪个客户真的很合拍？

Since you've been in this role, has there been a client you felt you just clicked with? Not because they bought a lot — but because your communication rhythm matched, they got what you were saying, and you didn't have to over-explain. — Work/Professional, Part 3 反思型话题

🎯 核心观点（选 2-3 个你最有共鸣的）

  • "合拍"和"业绩好"完全是两件事 — 有的客户买得多但每次沟通都很累，需要反复确认、反复解释；有的客户买得不多，但每次交流都很顺，这两者不能混为一谈
  • 合拍的核心表现是"不用把话说全，对方也能接住" — 说一半对方就懂你想表达的重点，不需要逐字解释每个细节，省掉大量来回确认的沟通成本
  • 本质是"沟通节奏对齐"，而不是"关系好" — 不是因为私下聊得来，是工作层面上双方对"什么重要、什么可以跳过"有一致的判断，这种默契很难强求，遇到了算运气
  • 升华：这种合拍感是这份工作里少有的"轻松时刻" — 大部分客户关系需要持续经营和翻译，遇到一个不用过度解释的客户，工作会突然变得像正常聊天一样自然

📝 Part 2 完整范文

Cue card: Describe a client you felt you really clicked with at work.

Yes, actually — there's one client I still think about, and it's a good example of how buying a lot and clicking with someone are two completely different things. They weren't even our biggest account, but working with them was, honestly, some of the easiest work I've done.

The clearest sign was that I never had to spell everything out. With most clients, I have to explain the reasoning behind a recommendation two or three times, in two or three different ways, before it lands. With this person, I could give a half-formed explanation, and they'd already be nodding — they just got where I was going before I finished the sentence.

I think what was really happening is that our communication rhythm just matched. I could tell almost instantly what actually mattered to them versus what was just noise, and it turned out they were reading me the same way — they'd skip the small talk I didn't need and go straight to the real question.

It wasn't about being friends outside of work, either — it was more like we were on the same wavelength professionally, even though we barely knew each other personally. And that's exactly what made it valuable: it wasn't personal chemistry, it was a genuine alignment in how we both processed information.

Compared to the usual back-and-forth I have with other accounts, conversations with them felt effortless. It reminded me that a big part of this job isn't the technical content at all — it's finding people whose thinking actually matches yours, and when that happens, the work almost takes care of itself.

💡 为什么能拿高分

  • 先划清概念边界 — 开场就区分"买得多"和"合拍",避免答案变成单纯夸客户业绩好,直接命中题目真正在问的东西
  • 用具体场景代替空泛形容词 — "half-formed explanation 对方就懂了"这种细节,比说"我们很合拍"更有说服力
  • 主动区分"私交"和"工作默契" — 展示思辨深度,不把职业关系简单等同于友情
  • 结尾升华到方法论 — 把个人经历上升为"找到思维方式匹配的人,工作会变轻松"这个更普遍的观察
